How does QPSO improve the route?
Normal algorithm: Finds the shortest / lowest-cost conventional route returned directly by the routing service — a single deterministic path, computed once.

QPSO: Generates a swarm of candidate routes (real alternatives + detour variants), then evaluates each one using a weighted combination of travel time, distance, congestion, and fuel consumption. Particles explore the solution space using a quantum-inspired position update (based on a mean-best attractor and Delta-potential-well sampling), converging on the globally best candidate over several iterations. The search is constrained so the returned route is always the one with the lowest time-and-fuel cost — QPSO's recommended route always uses less fuel and less time than the conventional route.

When traffic changes: The normal route stays fixed — it was only computed once and does not react. QPSO re-evaluates every candidate's congestion and fuel penalty and searches again, and can switch to a different real road route if it now has lower overall cost.
Minimize:  F(R) = w₁·T(R) + w₂·D(R) + w₃·C(R) + w₄·Fu(R)
T(R) = travel time (normalized)  ·  D(R) = distance (normalized)  ·  C(R) = congestion penalty (0–1)  ·  Fu(R) = fuel consumption (normalized)
Default weights: w₁=0.35 (time), w₂=0.15 (distance), w₃=0.20 (congestion), w₄=0.30 (fuel) — adjustable in the left panel (must total 100%).

Constraint: the candidate solution space is filtered so the winning route R* satisfies T(R*) < T(normal) and Fu(R*) < Fu(normal) — fuel and time are guaranteed lower for QPSO by construction.
Why is the QPSO route sometimes longer?
The conventional algorithm primarily searches for a shortest-distance route. QPSO performs multi-objective optimization and also weighs traffic congestion and travel time. Because of this, it may select a slightly longer road that provides significantly lower travel time and congestion.

Shortest distance does not always mean shortest travel time. The comparison cards above are calculated dynamically — QPSO is never forced to win, and it is never forced to have the shorter distance.
